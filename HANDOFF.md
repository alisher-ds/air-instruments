### Piano fix (owner: "it must be user-friendly, it does not press well", 2026-11-23)

No panel report came with it, so the cause is NOT proven, but I found a real design flaw by thinking about what the camera sees. Tests: **284 passing**, build passes, checked in the production preview with a finger that only gets SHORTER (no angle change): it plays, the ring fills, the Easy button is lit.

1. **The press signal was wrong for a hand facing the camera.** I measured the bend as the joint ANGLE in the 2D picture (`fingerCurl`). A palm facing the webcam bends its fingers TOWARD the camera, so the projected angle hardly changes; the finger only looks SHORTER. New signal **flex** = 1 - (tip-to-knuckle distance / the finger's usual length, in hand sizes: index 0.9, middle 1.0, ring 0.9, pinky 0.7, thumb 0.55). The press now takes the stronger of flex rise (`press: finger gets shorter by`, 0.08) and angle rise (`press: joint angle changes by`, 0.12), and the tip's speed down relative to the palm (`press: finger speed at least`, 1.2 hand sizes/s). **One strong signal (1.6 x its minimum) is enough**, or one at its minimum plus half of another (before: always two). All minimums are multiplied by `sensitivity` (`pianoEase`).
2. **Sensitivity buttons Easy / Normal / Strict** on the piano (x0.7 / x1 / x1.4, saved, default **Easy**).
3. **The ring on each fingertip fills while the finger bends** (full + green = it plays), so the player sees what the camera sees and learns how far to bend. (`PianoModel.meter`, `pressProgress`.)
4. **Which key:** the key under the fingertip where the finger RESTED just before the press (the tip drifts sideways while bending and the picture is ~100 ms old), then the key under the tip now. Tested.
5. **Bigger target:** keys are taller (62%..92% of the window height), a fingertip may hover up to 1 hand size above the keys, and a fingertip ABOVE the keys only counts for a black key in the middle 60% of it (else the white key behind it).
6. Release when the signal that caused the press has fallen back HALF-way (was 40%), loudness from the steepest flex / curl climb or tip speed (loudest wins). Hint text on screen now explains the gesture (palm to camera, fingers up, bend like pressing a button, press Easy if notes are missed).

If it STILL does not press well on his machine, ask for a **Copy report** while pressing: the next step would be to log, per finger, the numbers (`flexRise`, `curlRise`, `rel` are on every `on` event, and the ring shows the progress), or record with **R** and replay in a test. Not tried: using MediaPipe's z (depth) coordinate for the flex (the worker sends x and y only), a calibration step like the theremin's ("press each finger once"), and a bigger or fewer-keys keyboard (`octaves of keys` 1 gives 7 wide white keys).

**Keyboard.** 2 octaves from C4 (14 white + 10 black keys) in the lower third (y 66%..92% of the window, x 5%..95%); black keys 62% as wide and 62% as tall. Buttons "◀ Octave / Octave ▶" (and the panel: `octaves of keys` 1-3, `lowest octave` 2-5; saved) move/resize the keyboard. A fingertip may be up to 0.6 hand sizes above the keys and still count as "over" a key; black keys win where they are.

**Press rule** (PLAN.md 6.4, in `model.ts`): per finger, `fingerCurl` must RISE by `press: finger bend at least` (0.12) within ~160 ms AND the tip must move DOWN relative to the palm (`tipVelocityRel.y`, hand motion removed) at least `press: finger speed at least` (1.5 hand sizes/s): one signal at its minimum and the other at least half of its minimum. So moving the whole hand (no bend, no relative tip motion) and a curl wobble without the tip moving down both fail. Release: the curl falls 40% of the way back from its peak (`release when bend fell by`), or the hand is lost. Per-finger cooldown 50 ms. **Sympathetic fingers:** a finger is dropped if a NEIGHBOUR finger moved more than 1.6 times as much at the same moment (and really moved), so ring+pinky that bend together are not both played unless comparable. **Thumb ignored** by default (`thumb plays notes`). No presses while the palm moves faster than 8 hand sizes/s (a swipe) or while the hand pinches. Two fingers over the same key: only one plays it. All 10 fingers are independent (chords).
**Loudness:** the louder of (tip speed down) and (the steepest curl climb in curl/s: 1 = soft .. 7 = hard), 0.2..1. (The first version used the values of the frame where the press was DETECTED; with smoothing a fast press is caught only when it is already underway and came out quieter than a slow one: found by a test, fixed by using the steepest climb.) The `on` events carry `rel`, `rise`, `rate` for tuning.
**Sound:** 2-operator FM electric piano: sine carrier, ratio-1 modulator whose index starts at 0.6-3.2 (harder = brighter) and falls with a 0.45 s time constant (the "bark"), a quiet short tine partial at 5.9 x, 3 ms attack, a long exponential decay (low notes 3-5 s, high notes ~1 s), a key release damps in 0.08-0.4 s; at most 24 notes at once (the oldest is damped quickly); goes through the engine input (reverb). **Visual:** faint white keys, dark black keys, a lit key in its note colour with its name, a light bloom rising from the fingertip, faint hover highlight under each fingertip, fingertip rings (thicker while holding a key), the C keys named. Same cheap rules as the other instruments; hand-following things are drawn ahead by the real camera age.

**Honest limits / things to watch with real hands:** the tip must be over the key AT THE MOMENT the bend is detected (~100 ms after the real press), so a hand that is still moving sideways while pressing can hit the neighbouring key (draw-ahead moves the rings, the logic uses the measured tip); smoothing delays and softens fast presses (thresholds are guesses from synthetic bends: real curl change per press is probably 0.15-0.5, so `press: finger bend at least` 0.12 and `finger speed at least` 1.5 may need tuning); a camera that sees the palm from the side, or fingers pointing at the camera, gives little vertical tip motion (the rule assumes fingers pointing up in the picture, palm facing the camera); the thumb is off. Not built: sustain pedal, velocity from the key position, MIDI, a visible "how to" guide.

**Tunables** (panel group Piano): `octaves of keys`, `lowest octave`, `thumb plays notes`, `press: finger bend at least` 0.12 (now `press: joint angle changes by`), `press: finger speed at least` 1.5 (now 1.2; see the piano fix above), `loudest note at speed` 7, `neighbour finger moves x more = ignore` 1.6, `no notes while hand moves faster` 8, `finger cooldown` 50, `release when bend fell by` 0.4. Also `reverb amount`.

**Files.** `src/audio/theremin.ts` (+test: pure `thereminParams`, `ThereminSynth`), `src/instruments/theremin/model.ts` (pure logic), `visuals.ts`, `index.ts`, `theremin.test.ts` (26 tests), `engine.ts` (`thereminBus`), `config.ts` (group Theremin). Also in `main.ts`: **Start is now re-entrant safe** (a second click while starting used to close the camera under the first start and leave the start screen stuck on "Starting camera..." — found while testing).

**Playing.** Right hand (by screen position) height = pitch over 3 octaves of D (D3..D6), left hand height = volume (silent below 8% of its range, then a gentle curve); no left hand or no right hand = silent. Palm centre height, through a calibrated range. **Snap:** the pitch is pulled toward the notes of the scale: `snap` 0 = free glide, 1 = sticks to the notes (between two notes it is continuous and monotonic: near a note it moves slowly, near the midpoint quickly; tested). Default 0.5, scale = major pentatonic (own setting `thereminScale`, saved). **Glide:** the audio side ramps the pitch with time constant `glide` (60 ms). **Mute / articulation:** a pinch on the pitch hand mutes (15 ms release), opening it brings the sound back. 

**Vibrato** (`VibratoDetector`): the RAW palm x (average of wrist + 4 knuckles) over the last 500 ms, the straight-line trend removed (just moving sideways is not vibrato), RMS of the rest in hand sizes. Starts at 0.03 hand sizes, full at 0.12 (`wobble starts` / `wobble = full vibrato`), depth up to 45 cents (`vibrato depth`). It needs no frequency, so 14 fps tracking is fine (tested at 14 and 30 fps); the rate comes from zero crossings when there are enough samples (30 fps), else 5.5 Hz. **Auto vibrato:** if there is no wobble, on a held note (the sound is on and the nearest note did not change) it fades in from 0.9 s to full over 1.6 s, up to 30% of the depth (`auto vibrato on long notes`, 0 = off); a new note starts over.

**Calibration** ("show me your lowest and highest hand position", 2 s): starts automatically on the first use (nothing saved) and with the **Calibrate (K)** button / K key. A banner asks to move BOTH hands down and up; the 2 s clock starts when the first hand is seen; the range of each hand (min/max palm height as a fraction of the window height) is saved in localStorage (`air-instruments.theremin.v1`, sanitised on load). A hand that was not seen or moved less than 20% of the screen keeps the default (15%..85%) and the banner says so. The sound is silent while calibrating.

**Sound** (`audio/theremin.ts`): a sine + a triangle (22%, 4 cents detuned) -> lowpass (1.2 kHz quiet/low .. ~8 kHz loud/high, always above 2.5 x the note) -> gain; a vibrato LFO on both detunes; all controls ramped (no clicks); goes through the engine input, so the reverb applies. **Visual:** faint guide line per scale note across the screen with the note name at the right edge, the played note's line brighter; a glowing wave flowing between the hands (amplitude = loudness, number of waves + colour + flow speed = pitch, trembles with the vibrato); a disc and a tick on the pitch hand (hollow when muted), a vertical gauge next to the volume hand. Hand markers are drawn ahead by the frame age (drawing only). Same cheap rules as the other instruments.

**NOT verified:** everything with real hands: whether the calibration range feels right, whether the pitch is steady enough with ~100 ms of lag and 14 fps tracking, whether the wobble detection triggers on real shaky hands (the noise threshold 0.03 hand sizes is a guess from synthetic jitter; real raw landmarks jitter more at some distances: raise `wobble starts` if vibrato appears with a still hand), the sound itself (I did not hear it), the loudness relative to the harp/doira. Not built: pitch hand moving sideways to change something, a second scale UI (it is in the panel), recording raw landmarks (R records smoothed only).

**Tunables** (panel group Theremin): `scale`, `snap to notes` 0.5, `glide` 60 ms, `vibrato depth` 45 cents, `auto vibrato` 0.3, `silent below (left hand)` 0.08, `wobble starts` 0.03, `wobble = full vibrato` 0.12 (scale and snap are saved). Also `reverb amount`.

## Stage 3 — doira (done 2026-11-23)

Tests: **211 passing** (was 177). `npm run build` passes. Checked in the real browser (production preview) with synthetic hands: hits make sound (no console errors), one slam = one hit, the centre hit is a low thump and the rim hit a high-frequency click (in the analyser the zero-crossing rate was ~420 Hz vs ~2900 Hz), the drum draws. **I have never seen it with real hands.**

**Files.** `src/audio/drum.ts` (pure `drumVoices` + `DoiraSynth` that builds short-lived Web Audio nodes per hit; tested), `src/instruments/doira/model.ts` (pure logic), `doira/visuals.ts`, `doira/index.ts`, `doira/doira.test.ts` (26 tests, incl. 3 that run the REAL tracking pipeline with its One Euro smoothing), `engine.ts` (`drumBus`, `hitDrum()`, stats), a "drum" line in the debug panel (hits, `landmarks -> hit played` ms, target < 5; unmeasured by me), `config.ts` (group Doira).

**Hit rules** (PLAN.md 6.2, in `model.ts`): palm centre ENTERS the circle (or crosses it between two tracking frames) at >= 2.5 hand sizes/s, OR SLAM-AND-STOP: was >= 3.5 hand sizes/s in the last 150 ms and is now <= 0.5 x that, inside the circle. Whichever first. Speed -> loudness (0.12..1, full at 10 hand sizes/s). After a hit that hand must leave the circle or drop below 1 hand size/s to hit again; the speed history restarts there, so the fast tail of one strike cannot count as a second hit (found in the browser test: an enter-hit followed by a slam made 2 hits, fixed). Per-hand cooldown 90 ms. Two hands are independent. Prediction (`predictEnabled`, same as the harp) moves the palm ahead for the enter test.
**Zones:** distance of the strike from the centre -> 0 (centre) .. 1 (rim), crossfading bum (deep) and tak (sharp) between 0.4 R and 0.85 R. A hand coming in from outside is first seen just inside the edge whatever it aims at, so entering hits are pulled toward the centre (x0.65); a slam-and-stop keeps its true position. So: bum = hit in the middle (hover over the drum, slam down and stop), tak = hit near the edge or with the fingers.
**Finger taps:** fingertip over the drum (inside 1.05 R), relative speed (`tipVelocityRel`) >= 2.5 hand sizes/s on a rising edge, palm calmer than 1.5 hand sizes/s, 120 ms after a palm hit nothing; one tap per hand per frame (the strongest finger); per-finger cooldown 55 ms. Soft: quieter, shorter, higher.

## Render cost: what I found

Owner's measurement before the worker: render 12 fps while inference averaged 38-40 ms. Inference on the main thread blocks it for the whole call, so with a ~40 ms call and tracking at ~26 fps, the render loop was starved. **That is by far the most likely main cause, and the worker removes it.** Other things I found by reading the code and fixed:

| Finding | Fix |
|---|---|
| 3 full-screen passes per frame: clear fill, video, dim overlay fill | dim folded into the video draw (`globalAlpha = 1 - dim` over the dark background): 2 passes. The background fill is skipped when the video covers the canvas and dim is 0 |
| Pixel count: the canvas is CSS 1920x970; at pixel ratio 1.25 / 1.5 / 2 that is 2.4 / 2.8 / 7.4 million pixels to fill every frame, shared with the GPU that MediaPipe also uses | `maxDpr` slider (1 to 2, hard cap 2); the renderer re-sizes live |
| `fitTransform` object and the `rgba(...)` dim string rebuilt every frame | cached, no per-frame allocations in `beginFrame` |
| Hand overlay (drawn every frame while the debug panel is open, i.e. while the owner measures): 21 separate `beginPath/arc/fill` per hand + ~25 template strings per hand per frame | joints batched into 2 paths per hand, constant colour strings, one save/restore for all hands |
| Debug panel: stats rebuilt 10x/s, and `getStats()` (sorts samples) ran even with the panel hidden | 4x/s, and no stats work when hidden |
| `getStats` sorting arrays with `shift()` on each sample | ring buffer (`RollingStats`), no allocation per sample |

What I could not measure: the real GPU cost of the full-screen passes. I tried a micro-benchmark in the built-in browser; Chrome skips overdrawn work and the window is throttled, so those numbers were useless and I am not quoting them. The panel now shows the JS time per render tick (0.3 to 0.4 ms in my environment: the JS side is not the problem; the GPU side is what the `maxDpr` slider tests).

A bigger change I did **not** make (it touches the Stage 6 "record canvas" plan): put the video in its own `<video>` element under a transparent canvas, so the compositor draws and dims the video for free and the canvas only draws effects. Worth doing only if the numbers on the owner's laptop say the full-screen passes still hurt.

## Deviations / decisions to confirm

1. **Worker needs a small shim for MediaPipe (`self.import`).** MediaPipe loads its WASM glue with `importScripts()`, which does not work in a module worker, and the fallback (`import()`) fails with `ModuleFactory not set` (I hit this error). `trackerWorker.ts` therefore defines `self.import`, a hook MediaPipe itself looks for: it `fetch`es the glue file (same pinned jsdelivr URL) and runs it with an indirect `eval`. Needs a page without a strict CSP (GitHub Pages / Vercel have none by default). The alternative is a classic worker (`worker.format: "iife"`), but then dev and build would differ in how workers load, which is exactly the trap the AudioWorklet fell into. Tell the agent if you prefer that.
2. **"Grab newest when free" (`pickupOnFree`, on by default).** The brief says: if the worker is busy, drop the new frame. I do that, but with one refinement: when the worker finishes and a newer camera frame arrived meanwhile, that newest frame is sent immediately instead of waiting for the next camera frame. Still max one in flight, still no queue, older frames are dropped. Without it, a ~40 ms inference on a 33 ms camera would idle until the next camera tick and tracking would fall toward 15 fps. It is a live toggle: untick it in the panel to get the literal behaviour and compare.
3. All the Stage 0 deviations still stand (`?worker&url` for worklets, now approved and in PLAN; extra small files; 5 tips instead of 4; side by screen position).
4. ~~PLAN.md suggestions (tree, "Known risk", "Tracking loop")~~ **Done in Stage 0.7** with the owner's approval.
